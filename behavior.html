Real situations.
Quick responses.

Tap a situation to see what it might mean, what to do right away, and what not to do.

First thought

Don’t jump straight to “How do I stop this?”

Start with: “What just happened?” The same behavior can mean excitement, stress, confusion, overload, or a need for space.

Scenario cards

Pick the one that feels closest to what you’re seeing.

Scenario 1
Jumping in place + yelling a bit

Don’t assume it’s just “bad behavior.”

This could be excitement, waiting energy, or overload. Look at what happened right before it.

What it might mean

Excited, waiting, regulating, or starting to feel overwhelmed.

Do now

  • Stay calm.
  • Give them a little room.
  • Say what’s happening next.
  • Keep your words short.

Avoid

  • Yelling “Stop!”
  • Crowding them.
  • Assuming it’s automatically a problem.
Try saying

“You’re good. We’re almost up.”

Scenario 2
Walking away from the group

Don’t turn it into a power struggle.

They may need a break, feel unsure, or just not want the activity right then.

What it might mean

Break needed, confused, not ready, or needing space.

Do now

  • Follow at a calm distance if it’s safe.
  • Keep your tone easy.
  • Offer one simple choice.
  • Reconnect them to the next activity.

Avoid

  • Repeating “come back” over and over.
  • Grabbing them unless safety requires it.
  • Sounding frustrated.
Try saying

“Want a break, or want to come see basketball?”

Scenario 3
Gets quiet and doesn’t respond

More time — not more words.

They may be processing, unsure, overwhelmed, or getting ready to answer in another way.

What it might mean

Processing time, confusion, overload, or a different communication style.

Do now

  • Say their name once.
  • Give one short instruction.
  • Point or show what you mean.
  • Wait a few seconds.

Avoid

  • Rapid-fire directions.
  • Talking louder and louder.
  • Answering for them too fast.
Try saying

“Alex, stand here. Take your time.”

Scenario 4
Seems overwhelmed

Make the moment smaller.

Look for clues like covering ears, pacing, tighter repeated movement, frowning, or looking upset.

What it might mean

Too much noise, too much talking, too many people, or too much change at once.

Do now

  • Reduce the talking.
  • Use a calm voice.
  • Give space.
  • Offer a break, water, or a quieter spot.

Avoid

  • Touching without warning.
  • Forcing eye contact.
  • Giving too many directions.
Try saying

“Want a break?” or “Water or quiet spot?”

Scenario 5
Comes at you to hit, push, or grab

Safety first.

This is usually a sign of distress, frustration, overload, or a strong “no.” Don’t take it personally.

What it might mean

Distress, overload, frustration, fear, or a clear attempt to say “stop.”

Do now

  • Step back and give space.
  • Keep your voice calm.
  • Move others back if needed.
  • Get the coach or staff right away.

Avoid

  • Grabbing back.
  • Arguing.
  • Cornering them.
  • Trying to handle a risky situation alone.
Try saying

“I’m giving you space. Coach is coming.”

Important

If there’s any risk of injury, get a coach or staff member immediately. Safety comes first.

Quick reminder

These are starting points, not exact formulas. If you know the athlete, use what already works for them. If you’re unsure, ask the coach, parent, or the athlete themselves.

Pay attention first. Then respond. Small changes in tone, space, and wording can make a big difference.
