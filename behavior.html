Jumping + yelling

Start with the context.

Could be: excitement, waiting energy, self-regulation, or things starting to feel like too much.

Do now

  • Stay relaxed.
  • Give them some room.
  • Say what's coming next.
  • Keep it short.

Avoid

  • Shouting “stop.”
  • Crowding them.
  • Assuming they're misbehaving.
Try saying “You're good — your turn is coming.”
Walking away

Don't make it a standoff.

Could be: needing a break, being confused, wanting space, or being done for a bit.

Do now

  • Stay nearby if it's safe.
  • Keep your tone easy.
  • Offer one choice.
  • Reconnect with what's next.

Avoid

  • Repeating “come back.”
  • Following too close.
  • Grabbing unless safety requires it.
Try saying “Want a break, or want to come see basketball?”
Not responding

More time. Fewer words.

Could be: processing, confusion, overload, or getting ready to respond another way.

Do now

  • Say their name once.
  • Give one clear cue.
  • Point or show.
  • Wait.

Avoid

  • Rapid-fire directions.
  • Getting louder.
  • Answering for them too fast.
Try saying “Alex, stand here. Take your time.”
Overwhelmed

Make the moment smaller.

Could be: too much noise, talking, crowding, or too much change at once.

Do now

  • Say less.
  • Give space.
  • Keep your voice calm.
  • Offer water, a break, or quiet.

Avoid

  • Touching without warning.
  • Forcing eye contact.
  • Several people talking at once.
Try saying “Want a break?” or “Water or quiet spot?”
Safety first

Create space.

Could be: distress, overload, fear, frustration, or a strong “no.”

Do now

  • Step back.
  • Keep your voice calm.
  • Move others back.
  • Get coach or staff help.

Avoid

  • Grabbing back.
  • Arguing.
  • Cornering them.
  • Handling risk alone.
Try saying “I'm giving you space. Coach is coming.”
Immediate danger? Follow the coach or event staff's safety procedures and get help right away.
