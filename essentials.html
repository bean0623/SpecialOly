60-Second Essentials
Must read · before every session

Six things to keep in your back pocket.

No need to memorize a handbook. Take a minute, run through these, and you're good to go.

1 of 6 · Connect

Talk to the athlete.

Start with them — not the parent, coach, or person standing next to them. Say their name, say hi, and bring them into the conversation from the start.

Keep it simple:
“Hey Alex. Ready for basketball?”
Hey Alex!
Ready?
Start with them.
2 of 6 · Expression

Your face matters.

Some athletes pay really close attention to your face and body language. If you look tense, annoyed, or upset, they may think it's about them — even when it isn't.

A warm smile and clear encouragement can change the whole feel of the moment. And honestly, it's okay to make the positive stuff a little obvious.
Let the warmth show.
3 of 6 · Space

Give a little space. Watch their face.

Be close enough to help, but not so close that it feels crowded. Then watch what the athlete is giving you back.

Tight eyebrows, pulling away, looking tense, or a sudden change in expression can be your cue to slow things down and check in. It doesn't always mean the same thing — just notice it.
Watch the signals
A little room helps.
4 of 6 · Show

If the words aren't landing, show it.

Point to the spot. Pick up the ball. Do the drill once. Sometimes seeing what you mean is way easier than listening to another explanation.

Instead of repeating: “Go over there and pass the ball.”

Point → “Stand here.” → show one pass.
→
Show first. Then try.
5 of 6 · Wait

If they need a second, give them a second.

Ask your question, then hang back for a moment. They might be thinking, pointing, finding the words, moving, or getting their AAC ready.

Think:
Ask → wait → watch → respond.

You don't need to rush in and fill every quiet second.
Pause a beat
Give it a beat.
6 of 6 · Encourage

Bring the good energy.

A little encouragement goes a long way. Notice the effort, the communication, the small wins — not just whether the drill came out perfectly.

Celebrate the moment. A real “nice one,” a smile, or sharing in the win can matter more than over-the-top praise.
Nice one!
Celebrate the wins.
You're set.

Ready for the session.

You won't always know exactly what an athlete is thinking — and you don't need to.

Slow down. Pay attention. Stay warm. Work with the person in front of you.
