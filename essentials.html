02 · Words + actions

Say it + show it.

Pair short words with something the athlete can see. Your gesture can help give the words meaning.

TRY THIS Say less. Make the important part visible.
👍
“Yes.” + thumbs up
“Water?” + show bottle
“Stand here.” + point
03 · Be consistent

Use the same gesture for the same meaning.

Once a gesture means something, use it the same way every time. Consistency makes it easier to understand.

04 · Give it time

Give athletes time to react and respond.

Give one clear cue and leave time to respond. If repeating isn't helping, change your approach.

05 · Your energy

Always have a smile and show your good energy.

Some athletes notice facial expression and body language closely. If you look frustrated, they may think it's directed at them.

TRY THIS Smile. Encourage. Celebrate attempts. High five or fist bump if the athlete likes them.
😊
😊 “Nice!”
✋ High five?
✊ “Let's go!”
High energy ≠ loud
06 · Read the moment

What happened before can explain the behavior.

Jumping, yelling, walking away, or pushing away can mean different things. Check what happened just before.

TRY THIS Make what's next clear: “One more, then basketball.” “Your turn is next.”
