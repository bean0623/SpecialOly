60-Second Essentials
1 / 6
01 · Connect

Talk to the athlete first.

Use their name. Speak to them, not around them or only to the person beside them.

Remember: not speaking doesn't tell you how much someone understands.
Hey Alex!
Ready?
02 · Your expression

Your face matters.

Some athletes watch facial expression and body language very closely. A tense face may feel like you're upset with them.

Do: smile warmly and make encouragement easy to see.
☺
03 · Space

Give room. Watch the signals.

Stay close enough to help without crowding. Watch their face and body for changes.

Tight eyebrows, pulling away, or sudden tension = slow down and check in. Don't assume one signal always means one thing.
Notice changes
04 · Show

Show it. Don't pile on words.

Point to the spot, pick up the ball, or demonstrate once. One clear cue is usually better than a long explanation.

Example: point → “Stand here.” → show one pass. Ask before physically guiding or touching.
→
05 · Wait

Give them a beat.

Ask once, then wait. They may be processing, pointing, moving, speaking, or preparing AAC.

Ask → wait → watch → respond. Don't rush to repeat yourself or answer for them.
Give it time
06 · Encourage

Notice the wins.

Encourage effort and communication, not just perfect results. Let them know before you switch activities.

“Nice one.”  ·  “One more, then basketball.”
Keep it genuine. Every athlete is different.
Nice one!
Starting points, not rules — pay attention to the athlete in front of you.
