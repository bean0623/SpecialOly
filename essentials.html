60-Second Essentials
MUST READ · BEFORE EVERY SESSION

Six things worth remembering.

You don't need to memorize a manual. Take a minute before your session and keep these ideas in mind.

1 of 6
👋

See the athlete first.

Speak directly to the athlete. Use their name. Don't talk only to a parent, coach, or aide when the athlete is right there.

Try: “Hi Alex. Ready for basketball?”
2 of 6
🙂

Your face communicates too.

Some athletes may pay especially close attention to facial expressions and body language. A serious or frustrated-looking face can sometimes be misunderstood, even when you don't mean anything by it.

Keep it warm. Smile naturally, show encouragement clearly, and let your expression match the positive message you're trying to send.
3 of 6
◌

Give some space. Watch their signals.

Stay close enough to support the athlete without crowding them. Pay attention to changes in their expression and body language. Those signals can tell you when it's time to slow down or check in.

Tightened eyebrows or a suddenly tense expression
Pulling or turning away
Looking uncomfortable or overwhelmed
A sudden change in movement or behavior
These signs don't always mean the same thing. Notice them first, then look at what's happening around the athlete.
4 of 6
▶

Show, don't only tell.

Long explanations can be hard to follow. Demonstrate the drill, point to where you want the athlete to go, or use the equipment to show what happens next.

Instead of only saying: “Go over there and pass the ball.”

Try: Point → “Stand here.” → demonstrate one pass.
5 of 6
•••

Give them time.

Ask the question, then pause. Some athletes need extra time to process what you said, decide what they want, move, point, speak, gesture, or use AAC.

Think: Ask → wait → watch → respond.

Don't immediately fill the silence for them.
6 of 6
★

Encourage more than correct.

Positive energy can make a big difference. Let the athlete know when they're doing well, trying something new, or communicating what they want.

A smile, thumbs up, high five if welcomed, or a simple “Nice job” can go a long way.

Be encouraging without overwhelming them.
Done
✓

Ready for the session.

You won't know exactly what every behavior means — and that's okay. Start by slowing down, watching, and responding to the athlete in front of you.

Connect first. Then coach.

Back to guide

Every athlete is different. These are starting points, not rules for interpreting every athlete's behavior. When you're unsure, ask the athlete, coach, caregiver, or someone who knows their communication style.
