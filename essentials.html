60-Second Essentials
1 / 6
01 · Connect

Talk to the athlete first.

Use their name. Speak to them, not just to the parent, coach, or person beside them.

Remember: not speaking doesn’t tell you how much someone understands.
Hey Alex!
Ready?
02 · Your expression

Your face matters.

Some athletes read facial expression and tone very closely. If you look tense, they may think you’re upset with them.

Do: smile warmly, keep your tone easy, and make encouragement visible.
☺
03 · Space

Give room. Watch the signals.

Stay close enough to help, but don’t crowd. Watch face, body language, and tension changes.

Tight eyebrows, pulling away, or sudden tension? Slow down and check in.
Notice changes
04 · Show

Show it. Don’t pile on words.

Point, model, or demonstrate once. One clear cue usually works better than a long explanation.

Example: point → “Stand here.” → show one pass. Ask before physically guiding.
→
05 · Wait

Give them a beat.

Ask once, then wait. They may be processing, pointing, moving, speaking, or using AAC.

Ask → wait → watch → respond. Don’t rush to repeat or answer for them.
Give it time
06 · Encourage

Notice the wins.

Encourage effort and communication, not just perfect performance. Give a heads-up before transitions.

Try: “Nice one.” · “One more, then basketball.” Keep it genuine. Every athlete is different.
Nice one!
Starting points, not a script — pay attention to the athlete in front of you.
